# NicFree audit, 6 Nov 2026

Build 3 (1.0), pending developer release. I read every Swift file, ran the full test suite, and
wrote throwaway probe tests for the bugs I suspected (deleted afterwards).

**Verdict: release 1.0.** I found no crash and nothing that deletes data. The bugs below are
about accuracy and missed moments, and the hero number is right in every case I tested. Ship
them as 1.0.1 within a week or two, then build the product gaps.

---

## 1. Bugs, worst first

### B1. Can't log a slip for yesterday (high)
`HomeView.swift:303` always logs for **today**. The most common real case, "I vaped last night,
it's morning now", has no correct option. Logging it as today marks the wrong day. Logging
nothing leaves yesterday counted as free. The catch-up only fires after **two or more** days
away, so it doesn't cover this either. Accurate records are the whole product, so this is the
top fix.
**Fix:** the slip sheet gets the same Today / Yesterday / Another day pill as onboarding. Past
slips get added from History too.

### B2. Morning openers miss milestone notifications (high)
`MilestoneScheduler.swift:41-45`. Every rebuild removes all pending milestones, then schedules
only `milestone > freeDays`. Today already counts as free, so on the morning of day 7
`freeDays == 7`. If the app opens before 10:00, the "A whole week free!" note due at 10:00 that
day gets removed and never comes back. Checking the app in the morning is the normal habit, so
most people will miss most milestones.

### B3. Milestones fire, and show as reached, for a day that isn't over (high, rules 1 and 4)
The same "today counts" rule means the day-3 note fires at 10:00 on day 3. Someone who used at
9:00 and hasn't logged it yet gets "Three days free!". Path also ticks a milestone off at
midnight, and a slip later that day un-ticks it. That breaks the promise in
`PathView.swift:12` ("a reached milestone stays reached").
**Fix for B2 and B3 together:** count milestones in *finished* free days (exclude today). Fire
each one the morning after it's earned, and schedule it whenever its fire time is still in the
future instead of using `>`. Finished days never lose their free status, so nothing can be
un-reached. I'd also add milestones past a year (500, 730, 1000), because after day 365
Home and Path have nothing left to aim for.

### B4. Changing time zone hides slips (medium, confirmed with a probe test)
A `DayRecord.day` is local midnight stored as an exact moment. In another time zone, the
`Set.contains(cursor)` checks stop matching:
- the year grid and week row show that slip day as **free**
- longest run and current run change
- "Edit today's slip" and the thirsty sprout (`HomeView.swift:63`) stop finding today's record

The hero total stays right, because it only counts records in range. The probe logged a slip
in Dubai and read it back in London: the total matched, but the grid lost the slip and the
longest run changed.
**Fix (additive):** compare days by year, month and day, not by exact moment. Normalise each
stored day to the nearest local midnight when reading. Longer term, add an optional `dayKey`
(for example 20261105) next to `day`.

### B5. Tapping "Let's go!" twice can create two habits (medium, likely, not reproduced)
`OnboardingView.swift:314-326`. Nothing stops `finish()` running twice during the 0.4 s
transition. Every screen then reads `habits.first` from an **unsorted** `@Query`. Which habit
wins can change between launches, so reasons, quit date or spend edited in Settings could seem
to vanish.
**Fix:** a `finished` flag in onboarding, and sort the habit query (for example by
`quitStartedAt`). Then a duplicate is harmless and the same habit wins every time.

### B6. The craving timer counts time spent outside the app (medium)
`CravingView.swift:203`. Open the craving screen, switch to Messages, come back two hours
later, tap "It passed": that saves a 2-hour craving. Average length, "longest craving" and
"you've waited out N hours" are all inflated, and those numbers are what Insights is built on.
There's also no way out except "It passed" or "I used". An accidental tap has to be logged as a
beaten craving (inflating "Cravings beaten") or as a slip.
**Fix:** cap the hold at about 20 minutes (or stop the clock while the app is in the
background). Add a quiet ✕ that saves nothing if it's tapped within about 10 seconds.

### B7. Paid History shows only 40 events (medium)
`HistoryView.swift:313` uses `prefix(40)`. The paywall promises "every event". After a few
weeks, a paying user can't see or edit older slips.
**Fix:** a lazy list, or "Show more".

### B8. Granting notifications doesn't schedule anything yet (medium)
Milestones are rebuilt only when the free-day count changes (`HomeView.swift:123`). Turning
notifications on from the craving primer or from Settings schedules nothing until tomorrow's
count change, and only if the app is opened then.
**Fix:** reschedule right after permission is granted, or add the permission status to the
task id.

### B9. Opening Settings quietly changes the money saved (low)
`SettingsView.swift:243` rounds the weekly spend when it loads it. `onChange` (`:95`) then
writes the rounded figure back. A $25.50 spend becomes $26 without anyone touching it.
**Fix:** don't round on load, or skip the first `onChange`.

### B10. The daily line can change between launches (low)
`topTrigger` and `riskiestHour` (`CravingInsights.swift:66, 86`) break ties by dictionary
order, which is random per launch. `ObservationEngine` promises the line stays the same all
day.
**Fix:** use `triggerRanking.first` and a sorted tie-break for the hour.

### B11. Smaller issues
- The weekly chart and the "last week" trend leave out today (`CravingInsights.swift:105, 112`).
  Someone whose cravings were all today sees an empty chart.
- There's no lower bound on the quit date. A wrong tap on 1996 builds a 30-year grid in a
  non-lazy `VStack` (`YearGrid.swift:46`), which is slow. Use a `LazyVStack`, and maybe limit
  the date to 10 years back.
- A second "I used" on a day that already has a slip overwrites the first slip's trigger and
  drops its hold time.
- Restore says "couldn't find an earlier purchase" even when the person cancelled the sign-in.
- On iPad, two windows can each ask the catch-up question and add the same slip twice. The
  History list then has two rows with the same id.
- Reasons are saved in `onDisappear`, so they're lost if the app is killed while Settings is
  open.
- Settings can't change the product or the amount used. Picking the wrong one in onboarding
  can only be fixed by deleting the app, which deletes the history.

### Things I suspected that turned out fine
- Typing a weekly spend and tapping Continue straight away does save it. A probe UI test showed
  the Money saved chip.
- The total stays right across time zones (only the per-day views go wrong, see B4).
- Daylight saving time is handled, because days are stepped with `Calendar`.
- I found no banned words in the copy, no red, and no `print`.

### Before 1.1 ships: freeze the schema
There's no `VersionedSchema` or migration plan yet. Every change so far has been an optional
field, which SwiftData migrates without help. But the first real migration (a `dayKey`, more
than one product, CloudKit) needs a V1 that matches the schema you shipped. Define
`SchemaV1` now, while V1 is still exactly what's in people's hands. Rule 10 depends on it.

## 2. What would help people actually quit

Ordered by expected effect on quitting, not by effort.

1. **Make the craving screen use their own data.** NicFree already knows the average craving
   length. Show it right there ("Yours usually pass in about 4 minutes") with a countdown
   against it. That's the most persuasive sentence available at that moment, and today it only
   appears sometimes, on Home.
2. **If-then plans for the top triggers.** Insights says "a good time to have a plan ready" but
   there's nowhere to write one. Let people save a plan per trigger ("After a meal → walk to the
   shop and back") and show it on the craving screen when they pick that trigger. Planning
   ahead like this ("implementation intentions") is one of the best-evidenced tools in
   behaviour change.
3. **Get the craving tool one tap from the Lock Screen.** Unlocking the phone and finding an app
   is itself the trigger. Add a Lock Screen widget, a Control Centre control and an Action
   Button / App Intent that starts the craving timer. This is already in PLAN.md slice 5. I'd
   move it up.
4. **Actionable evening note.** Give "How did today go?" two buttons, *All free* and *Log a
   slip*, so it gets answered without opening the app. The catch-up question then rarely fires.
5. **Optional heads-up at their riskiest hour.** Off by default, built from their own data:
   "Cravings often show up around 3 PM. Your plan: …". The same pattern as the evening note.
6. **Set expectations for the first weeks.** Withdrawal is worst in the first few days and
   mostly fades within a few weeks. A sourced note helps people read day 3 as normal rather
   than as proof they can't do it. Sources (NCI, NHS) and the conservative end of the range
   only (rule 7). No body-healing timelines.
7. **Point to proven help.** Medication and nicotine replacement roughly double quit rates, and
   free text and phone lines exist (1-800-QUIT-NOW in the US, NHS Quit Smoking, and for young
   vapers the free *This is Quitting* text programme, which beat a control group in a trial).
   One "More help" page, sourced and by country. Nothing else in the category does this
   honestly, and it's on brand.
8. **Plan a quit date ahead.** Many people download before they stop ("Monday"). Allow a
   future date with a short "getting ready" mode, rather than counting free days before they
   start.
9. **A cutting-down mode for pouches.** Pouch users often taper first. That conflicts with "a
   record only on used days", so it needs a decision from you before any code. Flag only.
10. **Bring back the free-day celebration inline**, as a card on Home instead of a full-screen
    takeover, with "Log a slip for yesterday" next to it (this needs B1). That restores the one
    allowed place for cheer without the problem that made you switch it off.
11. **A shareable milestone card**: an image through the share sheet, not a social feature. It
    gives people a way to tell a friend, and it's free word of mouth.
12. **Ask for a review** (`requestReview`) after a beaten craving or a finished milestone, never
    after a slip. There's no review prompt at all right now, and ratings drive App Store ranking
    more than anything else you control.

## 3. How competitors make money

From App Store listings (US unless noted). Prices vary by country and date, so treat them as
ranges.

| App | Model | Prices seen |
|---|---|---|
| Kwit | Subscription + lifetime | $20.99/mo, $127.99/yr, $169.99 lifetime (€69–80 lifetime in the EU) |
| Smoke Free (23 Ltd) | Tiered "Pro" + coaching | Pro Bronze $6.99, Experts $9.99/mo, a "Money Back Guarantee" $49.99 |
| Smoke Free Forever | Subscription, 7-day trial | $4.99/mo, $29.99/yr |
| QuitVape | Subscription | $4.99/mo, $29.99/yr |
| QuitSure | Paid programme | $9.99 basic programme. Reviewers report much higher prices on other tiers |
| I Am Sober | Free + "Plus" subscription + lifetime | $17.99–39.99/yr, $64.99 lifetime, $2.99 community pass. Estimated $10K+/month |
| Pouch apps (PouchLess, Pouchly, Pouched, NOBUZZ, Velm, Quitory) | Weekly/annual + lifetime | Lifetime from $9.99 to $149.99. Most cluster at $35–60 |
| Quit Genius (now Pelago) | Sold to employers and health plans, paid on results | Not public. 55 clients by 2021 |

**What the category tells us**
- Nearly everyone sells a lifetime option alongside subscriptions, and lifetime prices are
  usually **$35 to $170**. At $9.99 NicFree is at the very bottom, priced like the junk tier.
- The money is in a **programme** (QuitSure, Smoke Free's coaching), not in charts. People pay
  for "a plan that gets me there", not for a history grid.
- Pouch apps are new, numerous and small, as BLUEPRINT found. Most push weekly subscriptions
  hard, so "pay once, no subscription" still stands out.

**What fits NicFree's rules (no subscription, no community, honest pricing)**
1. **Make the unlock worth more, then charge more.** At the moment the unlock is Insights and
   the full history. Add the things in section 2 that people will feel: if-then plans, Lock
   Screen and Control Centre tools, the riskiest-hour heads-up, a guided first two weeks, and
   later sync and Apple Watch. Then move to **$14.99–19.99**. That's still a fraction of Kwit's
   *yearly* price, and it's an honest comparison to put on the paywall ("Less than one month
   of Kwit. Yours for good.").
2. **A guided "First 14 days" programme** as the unlock's centrepiece: one short, sourced step a
   day (triggers, plans, withdrawal, what to do after a slip). This is what QuitSure charges
   for. Keep the core tracker free.
3. **Show the paywall at the moments the value is obvious, never at bad ones.** For example: when
   they write their first if-then plan, when they tap the widget setup, or a single mention on
   day 7. These are already allowed by PLAN.md and none is built. Right now the paywall opens
   only from the two locked cards. A gentle, skippable look at what's included at the end of
   onboarding is the biggest conversion lever in this category. It goes against your current
   "never during onboarding" decision, so it's your call. I'd test it only if day-60 numbers
   are weak.
4. **An optional supporter tip** (consumable, $2.99 / $4.99 / $9.99, "Water the sprout"), shown
   only after unlocking. Low effort, matches the indie and no-subscription story, and I Am
   Sober earns from a similar small extra.
5. **Launch pricing, honestly.** Use App Store Connect's price schedule: for example $6.99 for
   launch week, then $9.99, then the higher price once the programme ships. No fake countdowns.
   Offer codes for the non-consumable work for giveaways and Reddit or community promotions.
6. **Turn on Family Sharing** for `nicfree.unlock` in App Store Connect. It's already on in the
   local StoreKit file. Parents buying for teenagers who vape is a real audience.
7. **Later, B2B (the Quit Genius route).** Schools, universities and employers fighting
   teenage and student vaping buy in bulk through Apple's volume purchasing. It's a real
   channel, but only once retention data shows the app works. Not before day 60.

Things I'd **not** copy: weekly subscriptions, trials that roll into annual plans, "money back
guarantee" products, and community passes. They break rules 6 and 8, and they're exactly what
reviewers resent about Kwit and Smoke Free.

## 4. Suggested order

| Release | Contents |
|---|---|
| **1.0** | Release now, as is |
| **1.0.1** (about a week) | B1 slip for yesterday, B2 + B3 milestones, B4 time zones, B5 duplicate habit, B6 craving cap + ✕, B7 full events, B8 reschedule on permission, B9, B10, review prompt, `SchemaV1`, Family Sharing on |
| **1.1** | Craving screen with their average, if-then plans, Lock Screen widget + Control Centre + App Intent, actionable evening note, milestones past 365, change product in Settings |
| **1.2** | "First 14 days" programme, "More help" page, inline celebration, share card, price to $14.99–19.99, supporter tip |
| After day 60 | Decide on the onboarding paywall preview, CloudKit, Watch, multi-product, B2B |
